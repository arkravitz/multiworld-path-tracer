# Caustics research and implementation direction

Research cutoff: **14 November 2026**. Eight primary-source research/implementation families below; this is a targeted survey, not a claim that every 2026 paper has been found. Published results and project-specific recommendations are separated. The subsequent implementation is documented in [Fast caustics](fast-caustics.md).

## Recommendation for this renderer

**Engineering recommendation:** implement a small, explicit light-tracing caustic pass for fixed-world scenes first, then add a spatial photon gather for caustics seen through glass. Keep the existing camera path tracer for the remaining transport. This is a smaller step from the current Rust/wgpu implementation than full ReSTIR BDPT or a manifold solver, and it directly supplies the light–specular-chain–diffuse paths that camera continuation rarely finds. A sorted spatial grid can use ordinary compute buffers and integer atomics; it does not require NVIDIA-specific ray-tracing APIs. This is an implementation judgment, not a measured M1 speedup.

For sharp directly visible floor caustics, connecting photon landings to the camera avoids a density-estimation radius. For a floor seen through another glass object, that straight connection fails; a camera path gathering nearby photons at its diffuse receiver handles this important case at the cost of kernel blur. NVIDIA's current implementation demonstrates this division of work [5].

## Published work and implementation evidence

| Work | What it establishes | Implication here |
|---|---|---|
| **Specular Manifold Sampling, 2020** [1] | Samples constrained specular connections inside a unidirectional path tracer, including difficult specular–diffuse–specular configurations; its showcased remaining indirect caustic noise is outside its explicit sampling strategy. | Establishes the principled alternative when a sharp, direct caustic solver becomes worth the integration cost. |
| **Sample Space Partitioning and Spatiotemporal Resampling for SMS, Hong et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2025** [2] | Bounds Newton walks with tiles/trust regions, constructs per-frame seed priors, then reuses solutions with ReSTIR. Experiments use Falcor, RTX 4090, 1920×1080. Poorly covered light–object pairs can produce higher variance than SMS; differing bounce counts increase divergence. | Strong research reference for interactive manifold sampling, but requires a solver, inverse-probability estimation, priors and valid shift mappings. It is not a reservoir that can simply be attached to existing camera paths. |
| **ReSTIR BDPT, Hedstrom et al., 2025** [3] | Adds sampling-technique-aware extended path space, bidirectional hybrid shifts and separate caustic reservoirs. Reports approximately 50 ms/frame across test scenes on RTX 4090 at 1080p. Its lightweight variant keeps PT, NEE and light tracing while omitting general subpath connections. A practical temporal rejection heuristic introduces a small darkening bias. | Lightweight light tracing is an attractive intermediate milestone. Full reuse needs path records, probability/Jacobian accounting, pixel routing and history management. The quoted timing is not an M1 forecast. |
| **Online Photon Guiding with 3D Gaussians, Huang et al., 2024 preprint, revised August** [4] | Fits **per-light** 3D Gaussian mixtures to useful photon emission targets and supplies a directional sampling transform, scene-based initialization and adaptive light selection. Evaluation uses RTX 4070. The method addresses area-light parallax that simpler directional histograms miss. | Guiding improves where photons are launched after a photon estimator exists; it does not replace photon tracing/gathering. Start with a simpler caster-bound proposal and retain an unguided mixture component before adopting Gaussian fitting. |
| **NVIDIA vk_gltf_renderer, caustics beta, November 2026** [5] | A practical per-frame photon pass: camera splats for visible receivers, photon gathers behind glass, and explicit estimator partitioning. Uses adjoint transport on light paths, packed 20-byte photons and a counting-sorted hash grid. Integer compare-and-swap implements splat accumulation. The gather radius shrinks with accumulation but has a pixel floor, so it is a practical biased reconstruction rather than unrestricted asymptotic consistency. | Closest implementation reference for a limited prototype. Reuse its architectural ideas, not hardware timings or assumptions about this renderer's transport semantics. |
| **Guided ReSTIR FG+, Kern et al., EGSR 2026** [6] | GPU histogram photon guiding targets the visible view and combines it with photon final-gather resampling, hybrid shifts and backprojection. Official code uses Falcor/DXR; its README warns that refactored timings differ, photon overflow causes artifacts, and its implementation does not emit photons from environment/directional lights. | The most directly relevant newer photon/reuse direction after a basic photon pass. It still needs a substantial resampling architecture; the source implementation is not a Metal drop-in. |
| **Neural Progressive Photon Mapping, Benoist et al., Eurographics/CGF 2026** [7] | Published 30 March 2026. Predicts parameters of an analytical density kernel to retain progressive control over bias and variance. The paper explicitly changes kernel evaluation while leaving the photon mapping architecture intact. | A later reconstruction improvement, not a way to generate rare specular paths. It cannot turn the existing diffuse neural radiance cache into a caustic sampler by itself. |
| **Compatibility-Guided Neighbor Selection for ReSTIR, Junkins et al., HPG 2026** [8] | Improves spatial neighbor selection across pixel-space ReSTIR methods; reports 6–29% lower SMAPE for 2–5% incremental cost in its evaluations. Its heuristic does not solve disocclusion or test visibility. | Useful only after valid caustic candidates and reservoirs exist; it does not repair missing sampling support. |

## Geometry and transport constraints

The following are deductions for the documented renderer, not claims that the papers have validated this application.

**Flat triangles are an optical modeling choice.** The current mesh normals are constant within a face and jump at edges. More samples cannot turn the resulting faceted caustics into those of a smooth teapot. A manifold method can solve paths on individual facets, but moving between facets changes a discrete face sequence and can cross discontinuities. It needs robust seed/face handling; a smoothly parameterized analytic sphere or slab is the safer first solver target. Smooth shading normals alone do not create a curved geometric boundary and require transport corrections. Preserve geometric normals for entry/exit and sidedness.

**Ordinary NEE does not connect through a perfect dielectric chain.** A straight shadow ray through glass is not the bent specular solution. Making that ray transparent would change the estimator/model. More camera samples can eventually find finite-area emitters through glass, but a truly delta positional light cannot be hit by ordinary continuation. Keep these cases distinct when comparing demos.

**World switching changes the path space.** Fixed-world photon estimates can be composed for the image-blend mode, with each world's own estimator and history. A path that switches worlds during flight is a different stochastic transport process. Correct bidirectional use must represent world labels and transition probabilities, derive the reverse process and include any stationary/reverse-probability factors; merely tracing the forward switch rule from a light is not automatically the camera estimator's adjoint. Reservoir compatibility must also include world/history state. Keep the first caustic prototype restricted to fixed-world transport, and explicitly label that scope. Per-world photon maps alone do not establish correctness for mixed-world paths.

## Concrete implementation sequence and validation

1. Use one closed analytic glass object, a diffuse receiver and a finite area light. Establish raw fixed-world camera-path references and an index-matched control. Measure equal wall-clock error, not just equal samples.
2. Trace a bounded number of light paths, sample Fresnel interfaces with **importance/adjoint** throughput, and store/splat diffuse landings after a specular chain. Include emission position/direction PDFs, total emitted-photon normalization, Beer absorption, camera measure and visibility. Do not copy radiance-mode eta-squared factors blindly into the light pass.
3. Partition the covered caustic path family away from camera continuation, or derive correct MIS. Adding photons to all existing camera contributions doubles the overlapping transport. For a prototype, an explicit narrow path partition is easier to audit than full vertex connection and merging.
4. Add position/normal-compatible photon gathering through camera-visible glass, a finite-radius bias control and overflow reporting. A fixed radius stays biased; a radius floor limits final sharpness. Test a pane-over-floor case and two separated receivers to expose leakage.
5. Profile photon tracing, sorting, gathering and memory separately on **this M1**. Then consider photon launch guiding [4,6], temporal/spatial reservoirs [3,6], and finally SMS [1,2] if sharp constrained chains justify it. NPPM [7] comes after a working photon architecture.

All research performance figures above are author-reported on their own systems. No RTX-to-M1 ratio or claimed local convergence speedup has been established.
